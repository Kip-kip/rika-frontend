1. What you're actually buying

An aluminium window is four things: a frame (extruded aluminium, powder-coated), glass (clear, tinted, frosted, or double-glazed), hardware (hinges, rollers, handles, locks), and weather-sealing. When you compare quotes, you're really comparing these four. Two windows at the same "per m²" price can differ hugely in all of them — this is why the price alone never tells the truth.

📌 A "cheap" quote is almost always cheap on glass thickness, frame profile, or hardware — the three things you can't easily see from a photo.

2. Window styles — which does your house need?

  • Sliding — best seller for kitchens, lounges and shops. 2–4 panels, smooth rollers, no swing space needed.
  • Casement — hinged sash, tight seal, ideal for bedrooms and high floors.
  • Fixed pane — no moving parts, pure glass in a frame. The budget pick for top-lights and feature walls.
  • Bay / box — multiple angled lights; adds light and depth to a room.
  • Doors — sliding, hinged, or fixed; same finish/glass logic as windows.

4. Glass choices

  • Clear — maximum light; the baseline.
  • Tinted (light/dark) — cuts glare and heat, adds privacy; dark tint is the "premium" look.
  • Frosted — full privacy for bathrooms, ensuites, offices; diffuses light.
  • Double-glazed — two panes with a gas gap; best for insulation and noise (Premium tier).
  • Anti-burglar grills / mesh — physical security, added per window.

5. Finishes

Aluminium is powder-coated, so the finish is a durable coating, not paint. Standard: matte black, silver, bronze, white; custom RAL is possible. Choose a finish that matches your door/shutter hardware and exterior paint — a consistent finish across all openings is what makes a house look "done".

6. Measurement — the step everyone underestimates

A window is only as good as its measurement. A 5 mm error can mean a window that doesn't fit the opening or a gap that lets in water. A good supplier measures opening width, opening height, reveal (depth), and the condition of the existing frame for every opening. Rika's free measurement records all of this and gives you a firm price the same day. Never order from a photo or a rough tape measure alone.

7. How pricing works (and what to watch for)

  • Pricing is per m² of glazing, not per window — a big window costs more than a small one at the same rate.
  • Watch for exclusions: installation, transport, glass upgrades, grills and special sizes are often quoted separately. Ask what's in the number before you commit.
  • Prices should be a range or a firm figure confirmed at measurement — a single "ballpark" with no follow-up is a red flag.
  • A written quote with a reference number is the baseline. No reference = no accountability.

8. Installation & timeline

A typical full-house job runs 2–3 weeks from measurement to fit-out, done in phases so your house is never without windows. Expect: measure → fabricate → deliver → fit → seal → clean → walkthrough. Good installers level and plumb every frame, seal the reveal properly (this is what stops leaks), and leave the site clean.

9. Warranty — get it in writing

Workmanship warranty: 1 / 3 / 10 years by tier. The warranty should cover the frame, hardware and the fit. Glass breakage is usually treated separately. Get the warranty on the quote or a separate document — "we stand behind our work" is not a warranty.

10. Red flags when choosing a supplier

  • Price-only quotes with no breakdown and no reference number.
  • Refuses to do a free site measurement.
  • Can't show recent, local project photos.
  • No written warranty, or a verbal "don't worry" instead.
  • Pressure to pay a large deposit before the measurement and firm price.
  • "Same price as everyone else" — aluminium varies hugely in spec.
